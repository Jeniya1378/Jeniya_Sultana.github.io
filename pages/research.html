Research

My Ph.D. work uses wearable sensing and machine learning to understand safety and well-being in construction. Earlier work at Missouri State focused on context-aware language systems for smart homes.

Wearable sensing and machine learning for construction safety

My core Ph.D. research studies fatigue, safety and well-being during physically demanding construction tasks using wearable biosignals. It combines experimental design, signal processing and machine learning under real-world constraints.

  • EEG
  • EMG
  • PPG
  • Experimental design
  • Machine learning

GAN-based augmentation for biosignal analysis

Biosignal datasets from real workers are small and noisy. I use generative adversarial networks to augment them, improving how well models generalize in safety-critical, human-centered settings. This work led to our Construction Research Congress 2026 paper.

  • GANs
  • Data augmentation
  • Mental fatigue

Passive exoskeletons and multimodal sensing

Exoskeletons can reduce physical strain, but what do they do to attention and mental workload? I evaluate their physical and cognitive effects during construction postures using wearable sensors and structured protocols.

  • Exoskeletons
  • Cognitive load
  • Human factors
